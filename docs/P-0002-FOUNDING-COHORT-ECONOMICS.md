## 1. What changed from version 0.1, and why

Version 0.1 proposed a fixed Founding Cohort — the first 10,000 places — taking
35% of distributable surplus forever. The founder-steward rejected it on two
grounds, both correct, and the arithmetic proves the second one decisively.

**Run that structure at the scale this is actually aiming for.** If OURS ever
reaches 100 million members with €8B of annual surplus:

> Each of the first 10,000 receives **€280,000 per year**, forever, for having
> arrived early — **3,500× the claim of an ordinary member.**

That is not a cooperative. That is a landlord class of 10,000 people collecting
rent from 100 million, which is a precise description of the thing OURS exists
to replace. Version 0.1 would have rebuilt the enemy with nicer paperwork.

The second objection: **10,000 is not the ambition.** The registry names
LinkedIn, Instagram, X, TikTok, the app stores, Spotify, Uber. These are
operated at hundreds of millions to billions of users. A structure designed
around 10,000 people quietly concedes that this is a sold-out collectible for
insiders rather than an attempt to own a category.

## 2. The scale being designed for

| Members | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1,000 | The threshold rule fires on one target |
| 100,000 | Terms become negotiable with one incumbent |
| 1,000,000 | A category is genuinely contestable |
| 10,000,000 | The incumbent's growth model inverts |
| 100,000,000 | The scale the incumbents themselves operate at |

Every rule below has to still be fair and still be defensible at the bottom row.
That is the design constraint version 0.1 failed.

## 3. Proposed structure: declining multiplier bands

No fixed cohort and no fixed pool. Every member holds patronage units. Earlier
places hold more units — and because the denominator grows with the network,
the early advantage dilutes automatically instead of compounding into rent.

| Places | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 1 – 100,000 | **8×** |
| 100,001 – 1,000,000 | **4×** |
| 1,000,001 – 10,000,000 | **2×** |
| 10,000,001 and after | **1×** |

Distribution is `member_units / total_units × pool`, where the pool is a fixed
40% of distributable surplus at every scale.

### What that actually pays

Illustrative arithmetic, assuming a 40% pool and the surplus shown. These are
not projections; they exist to test the *shape* of the rule.

| Members | Surplus | Pool | Place #1 /yr | Newest member /yr | Ratio | First 100k hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000 | €10M | €4M | €40 | €40 | 1.0× | 100% |
| 1,000,000 | €100M | €40M | €73 | €36 | 2.0× | 18.2% |
| 10,000,000 | €1B | €400M | €143 | €36 | 4.0× | 3.6% |
| 100,000,000 | €8B | €3.2B | €228 | €28 | 8.0× | **0.7%** |

Compare the last row with version 0.1's €280,000 and 3,500×. The band structure
holds the early premium at a defensible **8×** and shrinks the founders' total
claim to **0.7%** of the pool as the network reaches real scale — which is the
correct direction, because at that point there are a hundred million people
whose participation is what made it worth anything.

## 4. The honest problem with all of this

**€228 a year is not a golden ticket, and no cooperative dividend ever will be.**

This must be said plainly rather than engineered around. Co-ops do not make
members rich. That is not a flaw in the model; it is the model. Any structure
that makes early members rich is extracting from later members or from the
enterprise — and at that point OURS is a startup with unusual marketing.

So if the pitch is "join early and get wealthy from dividends," the pitch is
either false or the institution is not what it claims to be. That is the
trade-off, and it does not have a clever solution.

## 5. Where the real money for members actually is

The dividend is the smallest part of what a member gets, by two orders of
magnitude. The large number is **what stops being taken.**

| If OURS replaces… | On annual activity of… | A member stops paying |
|---|---|---|
| the app store toll (30%) | €100,000 | **€30,000/yr** |
| the freelance marketplace (~20%) | €60,000 | **€12,000/yr** |
| the marketplace fee stack (~15%) | €200,000 | **€30,000/yr** |
| the booking commission (~17%) | €300,000 | **€51,000/yr** |

That is the golden ticket, it is available to every member rather than only
early ones, and — this matters — it is **not a security in any jurisdiction**,
because it is a discount rather than a return. Nobody has ever been prosecuted
for charging their customers less.

The correct headline is therefore not *join early and be paid*. It is:

> **Join and stop paying the toll. Join early and help decide which toll goes first.**

## 6. What earliness should actually buy

1. **The multiplier band** (§3) — a real but modest premium, capped at 8×.
2. **Priority of claim on what gets built.** Early notices set the build order.
   This is genuine power, it costs nothing to grant, it cannot be resold, and
   it is worth more to most people than the dividend.
3. **The permanent fee exemption**, if adopted — from the first product
   onward, for life.
4. **The record.** #000006 is not a financial instrument and never will be.
   It is the fact of having been there, which is not nothing.

## 7. What remains explicitly refused

- No purchase of a place, at any price, at any tier.
- No transfer, sale, gift or inheritance of a place.
- No secondary market, and no OURS-operated venue for one.
- No rank *inside* a band by ordinal — otherwise #000012 is worth measurably
  more than #000099, there is a price for it, and the number becomes tradable
  in practice whatever the rules say.
- No extra governance weight, at any multiplier. One member, one vote is
  constitutional and is not for sale at any scale.
- No payment for referrals, impressions or unretained signups.

## 8. Why this is probably not a security (and why that is not my call)

The distinction is not the size of the payment. It is:

| | Security-shaped | This proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Did the person pay to get in? | Yes | **No — free, permanently** |
| Can the position be resold? | Yes | **No** |
| Is the return for holding, or for doing? | Holding | **Doing** |
| Is there a market to speculate in? | Yes | **No** |

The classic US test begins with an **investment of money**. There isn't one.
That does not end the analysis — free distributions have been examined before,
and other jurisdictions test differently — but it removes the foundation the
structure normally rests on, and it is the difference between this and selling
positions on a ladder.

**I am not a lawyer and this is not advice.** Gate 1 exists for exactly this.

## 9. Case against this version

- The 8× premium may be too small to change anyone's behaviour, in which case
  the early incentive is doing no work and the complexity is unearned.
- Band edges are arbitrary and create a cliff at 100,000 and 1,000,000.
  Someone will be #100,001.
- A fee exemption for early members conflicts with charging later members the
  fees that fund the thing. This needs modelling before adoption.
- All of it is a promise until there is revenue, and promises are what OURS
  exists to be an alternative to.

## 10. Evidence and review required before publication

1. Securities review in the intended jurisdiction on the specific question:
   does a free, non-transferable, participation-based surplus share constitute
   an offering?
2. Cooperative-law advice on whether multiplier bands can exist as member
   classes in the chosen entity form.
3. Tax treatment of distributions across likely member countries.
4. Modelling of the fee exemption against actual operating costs.
5. A published answer to "what if there is never any surplus," written so a
   person can read it before they act.
