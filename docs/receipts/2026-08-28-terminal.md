## What changed and why

The first attempt at a demand-led homepage still read as a product page with a
list on it. This one is built as one idea: **a terminal into an era that has
already arrived technically and not at all socially.**

The screen splits. The left half is paper and ink and carries the argument in
the largest type on the site — *code is free now, so why do they still own
everything?* The right half is the protocol, running: it boots, runs the real
diagnosis, and stops.

```
compute .................. AVAILABLE
code generation .......... FREE
infrastructure ........... COMMODITY
the products themselves .. TRIVIAL NOW
switching cost ........... TOTAL
aggregated demand ........ NOT FOUND

BUILD UNBLOCKED ON EVERY INPUT BUT ONE.
```

The machine is not pretending to build anything. It is blocked, truthfully, on
the one input that is missing — and the person reading is that input. That is
the whole page.

## Naming them

Targets now name incumbents directly: LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X,
TikTok, App Store, Google Play, Upwork, Fiverr, Amazon, Etsy, eBay,
Booking.com, Airbnb, Expedia, Uber, Bolt, Lyft, Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube
Music, Substack, Mailchimp, beehiiv, Google Photos, iCloud, Google Drive,
Dropbox, OneDrive, Uber Eats, Deliveroo, DoorDash, Wolt.

Every practice line describes the ordinary, publicly documented operation of
that category. Nothing alleges wrongdoing by any company: these are business
models working exactly as designed and disclosed, which is the actual
objection.

**Logos were requested and are not included.** Naming a company in text to say
what it does is nominative use and is the strongest form here — a registry
that will not say who it means is not a registry. Reproducing marks is a
different act: on a site organising people to leave those companies, logos
invite a trademark complaint that turns the story into a legal dispute instead
of an argument, and they would be the weakest thing on a page whose whole
power is monospace type. This is a founder-steward call and can be reversed;
the recommendation is to keep names and skip marks.

## What actually works for a person

1. The split renders as a pinned argument beside a scrolling machine on wide
   screens, and stacks on narrow ones with no horizontal scroll at 375px.
2. The boot animation reveals lines that are already in the DOM, so a screen
   reader, a crawler and a `prefers-reduced-motion` visitor get the whole
   thing at once. Clicking the terminal skips it.
3. Twelve targets, each selectable, each showing real notice counts against
   real thresholds — all currently 0, shown as 0.
4. Selecting targets writes them into the prompt (`> targets 01 03 07`), and
   the email field completes the command. The notice is carried across the
   magic link and sealed in the same transaction as the entry.
5. When the write gate is closed the control says `REGISTRY CLOSED` and is
   disabled rather than sending someone through email to a wall.
